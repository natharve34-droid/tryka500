Fuelling

The goal: Harvey drops a small amount of fat and tones up — a mild deficit with high protein and the push/pull lifting kept in, so you lose fat, not muscle. Expect roughly a quarter to half a kilo a week; slower is more likely to stick. Doireann fuels to build — enough to have energy for the runs and get stronger, not to cut.

Harvey · macros by day

Carbs cycle with the work: more on the hard days, less on rest days. Protein and fat stay steady. These are starting points — hold your weekly average and adjust after two or three weeks based on the scale, the mirror and your energy in sessions.

Weekly average lands near 2,400 kcal — a mild deficit off an active ~89 kg maintenance. Protein ~2 g/kg protects muscle through the cut.

Per-session principles
Race week

Both of you: eat normally, nudge carbs up slightly for the last two or three days, try nothing new. Race morning — a familiar carb-based breakfast two to three hours before your wave, water with a pinch of salt, and nothing you haven't trained on.

General guidance, not a medical or dietetic prescription. If you want it dialled to you, a registered dietitian can personalise it.

Station splits

Why it's tilted this way

Your team time is set by the runs, and the runs are only as fast as whoever's struggling. So the split protects Doireann's legs for the running and loads the leg-wrecking stations onto Harvey. The ergs sit near even because they cost arms and lungs, not running legs. Retest at the week-6 and week-9 sims and adjust.

The course

TRYKA 500 · doubles · 8 stations · 8 × 500 m

A 500 m run before every station, then a 40 m sprint to the line. You run every metre side by side and only share out the station work. Cross the line together.

Movements

What each station asks, and how to hand it over cleanly.

Changeovers

Agree your swap points before each station and call them out loud mid-station. Teams lose more time hesitating at the handover than on any single rep. Fewer, planned swaps win — except burpees, where short alternating sets keep you both breathing.

Compromised running

Running on legs already loaded from a station. It feels like a different sport, and one of you will handle it far better than the other in ways you won't know until you test it — which is what the partner sessions are for.
